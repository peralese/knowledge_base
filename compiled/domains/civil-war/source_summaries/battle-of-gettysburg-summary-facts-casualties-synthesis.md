# Summary

The Battle of Gettysburg, fought from July 1 to July 3, 1863, is considered the turning point of the American Civil War. After a victory at Chancellorsville, General Robert E. Lee led his Confederate Army into Pennsylvania with hopes of forcing a political settlement and gaining international recognition for the Confederacy. The battle involved three days of intense fighting against Union forces under General George G. Meade. Key moments included Pickett’s Charge on July 3, which failed disastrously for the Confederates. The Union victory halted Lee's invasion and marked a significant shift in momentum towards the North.

# Facts

- **Dates**: July 1–3, 1863
- **Location**: Gettysburg, Pennsylvania
- **Union Commanders**: General George G. Meade
- **Confederate Commander**: General Robert E. Lee
- **Outcome**: Union victory
- **Significance**: Turning point of the Civil War; halted Confederate invasion

# Casualties

- **Union casualties**: Approximately 23,000
- **Confederate casualties**: Approximately 28,000

# Notes

- The battle is noted for its strategic and symbolic significance in the American Civil War.
- Pickett’s Charge, a major Confederate assault on July 3, resulted in heavy losses and is often cited as a crucial factor in the Union's victory.
