# Summary

The First Battle of Bull Run, also known as the Battle of Manassas, was the first major land battle of the American Civil War. It took place on July 21, 1861, near Manassas Junction, Virginia. The Union Army, consisting of approximately 35,000 troops under Brigadier General Irvin McDowell, aimed to strike a Confederate force of about 20,000 led by General P.G.T. Beauregard. Despite initial Union advances and defensive successes at Henry House Hill, the Confederates rallied and launched a counterattack that broke the Union lines. The battle concluded with a chaotic retreat by Union forces toward Washington, D.C., marking a decisive Confederate victory.

# Key Insights

- **Date and Location**: The First Battle of Bull Run occurred on July 21, 1861, near Manassas Junction, Virginia.
- **Combatants**: Approximately 35,000 Union troops faced about 20,000 Confederate soldiers.
- **Outcome**: The Confederates won the battle, leading to a disorganized retreat by the Union forces.
